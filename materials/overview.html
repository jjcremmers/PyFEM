Materials

PyFEM provides material models for linear and nonlinear analysis, including elastic, elastoplastic, anisotropic, cohesive-zone, and failure models. A material model defines the constitutive behavior that relates stress to strain, or traction to displacement jump for cohesive elements. Materials are configured within element definitions in the .pro input file. Each element group can have its own material definition, or materials can be defined globally and referenced by name.

Basic syntax

The material block is nested inside an element definition:

ElementGroup = {
  type = "SmallStrainContinuum";
  material = {
    type = "PlaneStress";
    E    = 100.0;
    nu   = 0.3;
  };
};

The type parameter selects the material model. The remaining parameters define its material properties, such as Young’s modulus E and Poisson’s ratio nu. Refer to the individual material pages for required parameters, units, assumptions, and examples.

Continuum Constitutive Models

These models describe the constitutive response of bulk materials under elastic, plastic, anisotropic, thermal, and multiscale assumptions. They cover linear elasticity, isotropic and kinematic hardening, finite-strain behavior, damage, viscoelasticity, viscoplasticity, composite and sandwich materials, and specialized crystal or micromechanical models. Select a model according to the material behavior, strain regime, internal variables, and element formulation used in the analysis. The individual pages document the supported parameters and their physical interpretation.

Cohesive Zone Models

Cohesive constitutive models describe the local relation between the displacement jump across two adjoining surfaces and the tractions transferred between them. The response generally includes an initial elastic or penalty regime, followed by damage and softening as the interface opens or slides, and may end in complete decohesion. Parameters such as the peak traction and fracture energy control the onset and evolution of failure. These models are used for delamination, adhesive failure, peeling, and other debonding processes. They must be assigned within the material block of the Interface element, which supplies the interface kinematics and transfers the resulting normal and tangential tractions to the adjacent continuum parts.

Failure Models

These models evaluate stress-based or composite-specific failure criteria. They identify the onset of failure by comparing computed stresses or strains with material limits, and can distinguish common modes such as tension, compression, shear, or interaction between them. The collection includes general stress and strain limits, von Mises yielding, and composite criteria such as Tsai–Wu and LaRC03. Failure models are used to assess a simulation’s state and should be selected consistently with the material orientation, loading conditions, and intended failure definition.
