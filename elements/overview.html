Elements

PyFEM provides a comprehensive library of finite element formulations for structural and solid mechanics analysis. Elements define the kinematic assumptions, interpolation functions, and integration schemes that transform continuum mechanics equations into discrete algebraic systems.

Elements are defined by creating named element groups in the .pro file. Each group specifies the element type and its material properties.

See documentation for configuration examples and details.

Continuum Elements

Continuum formulations describe the deformation and constitutive response of solid bodies under small- and finite-strain assumptions. They are suitable for bulk materials in two or three dimensions, including axisymmetric and thermo-mechanical problems. The available formulations cover small- and finite-strain kinematics, thermal expansion and conduction-related coupling, and surface heat-transfer effects. Choose an element according to the assumed strain regime, geometry, field variables, and material behavior. The individual pages provide the supported degrees of freedom, integration details, required material properties, and example .pro definitions.

Structural Elements

Structural formulations model beams, trusses, plates, and shell structures. They are intended for slender members and surface-like components where a reduced structural model is more efficient than a full continuum mesh. PyFEM includes linear and nonlinear beam elements, trusses, Kirchhoff and Reissner–Mindlin plates and shells, and solid-like shell formulations. These elements can represent axial deformation, bending, torsion, transverse shear, and geometric nonlinearity. Select a formulation according to the component’s geometry, kinematic assumptions, and required degrees of freedom. Consult the individual element pages for input properties and example .pro definitions.

Special Elements

Special formulations include interface, cohesive-zone, and spring elements. These elements represent localized behavior that is not efficiently described by standard continuum or structural elements. Interface and cohesive elements can model separation, contact-like interactions, and damage along surfaces or between material regions, while spring elements provide discrete stiffness or connector behavior. They are often combined with other element types to model joints, supports, delamination, or other localized mechanical effects. Consult the individual pages for connectivity, degrees of freedom, and input syntax.
